## Context

The game's camera is five whole numbers (`Medium.x, y, z`, the yaw `xz` and the pitch `zy`, in degrees; no roll), placed once a tick by the view the player chose (chase, around the car, watching it go by). The race reads what that camera sees: a car out of view finishes a repair hoop at once, one in view waits for the animation, and the HUD's arrows follow what is on screen. Frames are drawn between ticks from the camera of the tick before and the tick now (`renderScene` on the processor, `PoseSpan` for the kept scene the card places), with the fractions between whole numbers kept exactly (`Precise.cam*`, `FramePose`). The view is recorded with the race (two bits of each tick's keys).

## Goals / Non-Goals

**Goals:** the two requirements of `first-person`, each scenario checked (the lap waits for the owner); only the picture changes (the race, its recording and the race camera are the chase view's); smooth at any frame rate; the card draws the vignette; nothing allocated per frame or tick; nothing at all with the switch off.

**Non-Goals:** a cockpit interior (the game's cars have none: their models are shells); rolling the view with the car (the game's camera cannot roll, and adding a roll to every drawing path for one view is out of proportion): the head keeps the horizon level, as a driver's head does.

## Decisions

1. **Drawing only.** With the switch on, the view key cycles chase, around, watch, helmet. In the helmet view the race's own view is the chase view: placed by `Medium.follow` each tick, recorded as view 0, read by the race. Only the frames are drawn from the helmet's camera. So a race in the helmet view is the race in the chase view, and its replay shows the chase view. Each player has their own (split screen). The helmet is reset with the views when a race starts, as the views are.
2. **The head** is a damped spring per axis (forward and back, side to side, up and down), driven by the car's acceleration in its own frame, measured from its velocity tick to tick (the game's gravity, 7 units a tick a tick, is 1 g): settled, one g moves the head 9 units forward or back, 7 sideways, 5 up or down; the natural frequency is about 1.6 Hz and the damping ratio 0.45, so it overshoots by about a fifth when a force ends. Worked out in 4 steps a tick; limits on each axis; a crash or a respawn (a jump in place) resets it. Braking throws the head forward and nods it down a little, acceleration back, corners to the outside, a landing down (the car's sudden upward acceleration), the air up a little (weightless).
3. **Looking into the corner.** The head turns toward where the car is going: its turn rate (degrees a tick) and its drift (the angle between where it points and where it moves), smoothed, at most 20 degrees.
4. **The eyes.** From the car's model: with glass, in the middle of the glasshouse along the car, a third of the way down from the roof line; with none (an open car), just above the body at its middle. The head's offset is added in the car's frame, through the car's full pose. The view turns with the car's yaw; its pitch follows the car's pitch through a sine, at most 55 degrees, so a flip never turns the picture upside down.
5. **Exact and smooth.** The camera is worked out as real numbers and handed to the frame as whole numbers and fractions (`Precise.cam*` on the processor, new fractions in `PoseSpan` for the card), so the head moves smoothly at any frame rate, as the cars do. The game's projection turns the camera about a point 50 units ahead of the eyes (`Medium.cz`); the camera's numbers are worked out from the eyes accordingly.
6. **The car seen from inside.** The player's own car is drawn from the eyes, but only its polygons that lie wholly ahead of the eyes and below them and are not glass: the bonnet, the front wings and wheels. The game draws a polygon that reaches behind the eyes stretched across the picture (it does not cut polygons at the eyes), so the roof, the sides and the back are left out, only in that player's helmet frames. Below the bonnet's back edge the eyes would see through the car (its floor, the insides of the wheel arches, the road under it), so a dashboard hides it: one dark polygon across the view at the foot of the windscreen, at least 66 units ahead of the eyes (whole even with the head thrown forward the farthest), its top level with the top of the bonnet, drawn right after the car through `Canvas.onCar` so it moves with the car's smooth pose and goes into the kept scene. Extras drawn on the car (a flapping bonnet, a cracked windscreen) are seen as they are.
7. **Order.** The helmet's frames sort the objects and their polygons by the depths the helmet's own last frame measured (`PaneDepths`), as split screen's panes do; the race's depths (measured from the chase camera by the draw step) are put back after every frame, as always.
8. **The vignette.** The edges of the view darken with the g-force the head feels (its offset on each axis in g), from 0.35 g up to 55% at 1.8 g. Drawn by the card after the scene and before the HUD: one full-picture pass with a small shader (`VIGNETTE_FS`), blended, not dithered, in each pane whose player is in the helmet. In software, a gradient image drawn over the scene. Its strength goes with the kept scene and is interpolated between the ticks like the camera.
9. **A test property.** `-Dnfm.helmetview=on` starts every race in the helmet view (with the switch on), for the hidden tests: `verify-races` with it plays the recorded races drawn from the helmet and proves them unchanged; the autotest's screenshots show the view.

## Performance

Per tick and player: a few dozen operations. Per frame: the camera worked out twice (start and end of the span), a comparison per car polygon drawn, and one full-picture pass for the vignette (a few multiplications a pixel) only while it shows. Nothing is allocated after the race starts.

## Races stay identical

The race camera, the recorded view and the draw step are the chase view's; the helmet's frames put back everything drawing changes, as every frame does. `test\verify-races.ps1 -Features looks` with `-Dnfm.helmetview=on` plays the 20 recorded races drawn from the helmet.
