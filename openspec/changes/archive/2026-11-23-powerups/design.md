## Context

A race is one deterministic object graph played at 19 ticks a second (`advanceRace`): the cars collide, drive (`Madness.drive`), are recorded for the replay, the standings are worked out, the computer drivers choose their keys. Run-ahead plays the next tick ahead and puts everything back (`RaceSnapshot`); the verifier plays recorded races from their keys and compares a hash per group of the race state after every tick (`RaceState`, `RaceLog`). Extras so far only read the race, through a copy (`RaceView`) after real ticks. Powerups are the first extra that changes races; the framework's decision 8 says how: a tick call inside `advanceRace`, state visited only while the extra runs, dice of its own, the recording noting it.

## Goals / Non-Goals

**Goals:**
- Every powerup requirement, behind the `powerups` switch, in split screen as well, for the computer cars too.
- A race without powerups is exactly as before: the same hashes, the same dumps, the same field layout, the same keys recorded.
- Run-ahead, the verifier and the replays keep working with powerups on: all their state is captured, drawn from its own dice, and the recording says they were on.
- No allocation per tick or frame after the race starts.

**Non-Goals:**
- Recorded races with powerups (`test\races-extras`) and their checks: they wait for the testing harness the owner will specify (owner, 23 Nov, 16:07 UTC).
- Online play: run-ahead and rollback already cover it.

## Decisions

1. **Two halves.** `PowerupRace` is the race part: plain arrays, written only inside the race tick, reading and moving the cars through `RaceTick`, an interface `FeatureBridge` implements over `Madness`, `ContO`, `Control`, `CheckPoints` and `Trackers` (package-private there). `PowerupsFeature` is the look and the sound: it reads `PowerupRace` after real ticks only (never a run-ahead tick), like every other extra reads `RaceView`, and never writes it. Rejected: the race part in `nfm.lit` (`code-health`: each feature in its own folder).
2. **The tick hook.** `Features.raceTick(tick)` runs in `advanceRace` right after every car has driven and before the replay records them, the standings and the computer drivers, only while the race proper runs (`starcnt == 0`). The cars it moves are recorded and ranked as they end the tick. The boost is one line in `Madness.drive`, after the game's own boost (the stunt power, 40 at least, three quarters for a person below full power): `boost += FeatureBridge.raceBoost(im)`, which returns `0F` without a race extra; adding `0F` to a positive float leaves it bit for bit the same.
3. **State.** Every value the race part changes is in fixed arrays saved and restored by one method, `sync(RaceInts)` (`x = s.value(x)`, `s.contents(array)`), so saving and restoring cannot drift apart (as `ContO.syncRaceState` does with `StateBuffer`). `RaceSnapshot.capture` saves it after the race's own values and `restore` puts it back; the complete snapshot keeps a copy too and `differences()` reports `extras` if it changed. `RaceState.visit` adds a group `extras` after `random`, only while a race extra runs (`RaceState.groupCount` grows by one then), so a race without one walks, hashes and dumps exactly the values it did. The boxes' places, worked out once when the stage loads, are not state.
4. **Dice.** The race part's own SplitMix64, seeded from `RaceRandom.derive(seed, STREAM_EXTRAS)` (stream 7) when the stage loads, its 64 bits two values of the state. The race's stream (`World.random`) is never drawn from.
5. **Which race extras play.** Decided when the stage loads (`Features.raceStarting`): the switches, or, while a recording plays back (the verifier), the recording's list. `RaceLog` format 7 writes that list (ids, comma-separated) after the format-5 fields; 3 to 6 are read without it, as races with none. The custom game's branch has a format 6 of its own (its choices after the format-5 fields): on merge its field goes before this one and both are read from 7.
6. **The key.** `KeyBindings.USE_POWERUP` (12; the game's own keys keep their numbers, a saved table of 12 reads as before with Ctrl added), Ctrl by default; `SplitKeys` gives Player 1 R and Player 2 L or number pad 2; `Gamepads` presses it with the right stick's button (legacy code 99, `c`). It is latched once a tick into `Control.power` with the driving keys, only while a race extra plays (so a race without one records the same keys), and run-ahead re-plays when it changes (`heldKeys` bit 512). `RaceState` leaves `power` out of the reflected driver (`CONTROL_SKIP`) and visits it in `extras`; the recording's input byte carries it as bit 64 (`KEY_POWER`), which the canonical `input` masks out. A press is used on the tick it starts (`usedKey` remembers last tick's).
7. **Boxes.** At each checkpoint (every second one if there are more than 16) a row of three boxes 170 units apart across the road (the gate's direction: `typ` 1 across x, 2 across z), 70 units over the road there (the top of the flat collision box under it nearest the checkpoint's height, else the ground at 250). A car whose middle comes within 60 units plus 0.4 of its radius across and 160 up or down takes one if it holds nothing and is not wasted; the box comes back 76 ticks later (4 s).
8. **What a box gives.** By place (`CheckPoints.pos`, the cars ahead): nitrous 40, dynamite 30, magnet 30 in a hundred; the leader never the magnet (nitrous 57, dynamite 43); the last car nitrous 40, dynamite 20, magnet 40.
9. **Nitrous.** 38 ticks (2 s) of 60 more boost, then fading to none over 19 ticks: by the game's own formulas the car accelerates harder in every gear and its top speed rises (at full power by 31%, a person below full power by up to 47%). Only with the throttle held and on the ground, as the game's boost works.
10. **Dynamite.** 38 ticks after the pickup the stick leaves the car's nose at its speed plus 60 units a tick forwards and 16 up, falling 1.4 a tick per tick: it blows up on the ground (the same ground as the boxes'), on a car (within 90 units; the thrower's own only after 12 ticks) or after 45 ticks. Cars within 520 units are hit by `s = 1 - d/520`: each wheel pushed away from the blast by 55·s and up by 30·s to 45·s (more for the nearer wheels, so the car tips), damaged by a quarter of its maximum times s, its screen shaken (`shakedam`). On the ground it leaves a crater.
11. **Magnet.** Usable with a car one place ahead (`pos` one less) not wasted; else it stays held and the player is told. For 57 ticks (3 s) the car is moved toward that car by up to 9 units a tick (a tenth of the gap beyond 260 units, so it closes in without ramming) and has 25 more boost; it stops early if the target is wasted.
12. **Computer cars.** Nitrous on the ground with the throttle held, not steering, above 60% of top speed: a 12% chance each tick. The magnet with a car ahead between 400 and 3000 units: 15% each tick. Dynamite throws itself.
13. **Events.** A ring of 32 events (kind, car, powerup, place) and their count are part of the state; the look and sound play those after the count they saw last, after each real tick, so an undone run-ahead tick is never seen or heard.
14. **The look.** Items through the framework's queue: boxes and the stick at their depth, the held powerup and the flame on the car, craters on the ground, puffs as `Particles`. Boxes spin and bob smoothly: each corner is given its move to the next tick (`Canvas.moving`). Polygons of a solid are drawn back to front with the faces turned away left out. Explosions: a fireball of glowing orange and yellow puffs, dithered, growing fast and fading in 0.6 s; 40 smoke puffs that rise, spread and stay about 8 s, pushed aside by a car passing through them (`Particles.push`, the car's motion and outwards); clods and sparks. A crater: a scorched disc, then a bowl 18 units deep inside a raised lip, its faces shaded by their slope and darkened by soot, kept for the race (16 at most). The flash: the game's own white start flash (one frame, see `pendingWhiteFlash`) when a stick blows up within 6000 units of a player's car and the `flash` switch is on.
15. **Sound.** Synthesized once (fixed seeds): the pickup (a rising chime), the three hums (looped), the nitrous burst, the magnet's clunk, the throw and the blast. The 3D sound: one-shots at the car (`Sound3D.carSound`), the blast at its place (`Sound3D.pointSound`, a voice held at a world point), and each car's hum from a looping source of its own that follows the car (`Car.hum`, published with the engines). Classic: the same clips, played on their own through `FeatureBridge` (never through the game's `SoundManager`, so its sound commands, which the recorded races check, stay as they were): a player's own pickup, uses and hum, and blasts within 6000 units of a player, unless the player turned the sounds off.
16. **The HUD.** On a pickup a person is told in the game's message line ("Nitrous! Ctrl to use", "Dynamite! Thrown in 2 seconds", "Magnet! Ctrl to use"), with their own key named; a magnet with nobody ahead says so.

## Risks / Trade-offs

- [The magnet moves the car straight, so it can be pushed into a wall] → at most 9 units a tick; the game's collision puts it back as after a scrape.
- [A blast can waste a car] → as in the original, wasting is part of the game; a quarter of the maximum at the centre wastes only a car already badly damaged.
- [The flash is the full white of the start] → it lasts one frame, only for blasts near a player, and the `flash` switch turns it off.
- [Format 7 and the custom game's 6] → noted for the merge (decision 5).
